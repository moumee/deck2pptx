The problem

The round trip no longer fits the budget

Interactive AI features promise an answer before the user notices a wait: roughly 150 ms to the first token.

  • Physics sets the floor. A 9,000 km round trip costs about 90 ms in fibre before a single FLOP runs.
  • Queues amplify it. Centralised GPU pools run at 70-85% utilisation, so p99 queueing adds 40-120 ms.
  • Jitter is visible. Users notice variance in time-to-first-token long before they notice the mean.
  • Egress is the hidden bill. Shipping prompts, images and embeddings to a few regions was 31% of serving cost.
  • Residency rules tighten. Eleven of our top 30 markets now require in-country processing for some inputs.
  • Power is the real cap. A PoP rack gives us 12 kW; every watt per inference is a trade against capacity.

Architecture

One request, five hops, under 80 ms

Anycast steers the user to the nearest PoP; everything after that is a local decision.

  1. Anycast landing

    BGP anycast lands the connection at the nearest healthy PoP and TLS 0-RTT resumes the session. 9 ms median.

  2. Admission and routing

    The router reads tenant, model and priority, then picks a replica by queue delay, not round-robin.

  3. Prefix and semantic cache

    KV-prefix reuse and an embedding cache answer or shorten 18% of prompts before an accelerator is touched.

  4. Quantized runtime

    AWQ INT4 7B model on NPU or GPU, with continuous batching and paged KV memory.

  5. Overflow and fallback

    When queue delay passes 12 ms, the router spills to the regional GPU pool and tags the request for replay.

Choose edge when

p99 time-to-first-token must stay under 150 ms, or inputs must be processed in-country.

Choose regional when

the model is larger than 13B, or traffic is global, spiky and tolerant of 300 ms.

Choose on-device when

inputs are private by nature and a 3B model is good enough for the task.

# edge-deploy.yaml: one manifest per model, rolled out per PoP cohort
apiVersion: edge.lattice.example/v2
kind: ModelDeployment
metadata:
  name: assist-7b-int4
spec:
  artifact: registry.lattice.example/assist/7b@sha256:9f2c...e41a
  quantization: { scheme: awq-int4, group_size: 128 }
  placement:
    tier: pop
    min_replicas: 2
    fallback: regional-gpu
  batching: { mode: adaptive, max_wait_ms: 6, max_batch: 32 }
  autoscale:
    signal: queue_ms_p95
    target: 12
    cooldown_s: 45
  rollout: { strategy: canary, steps: [1, 5, 25, 100], bake_min: 20 }
L8

Quantization

AWQ INT4, group size 128. Costs 0.6 pt on the eval suite and cuts weight memory about 3.8x.

L13

Adaptive batching

Waits at most 6 ms to fill a batch of 32. This is the setting behind the knee on the previous slide.

L15-17

Autoscale on queue delay

Scale on p95 queue time, not CPU. Target 12 ms with a 45 s cooldown to avoid flapping.

L18

Canary rollout

1, 5, 25, 100% of PoPs with a 20 min bake per step. The SLO guard auto-reverts on breach.

Lessons

What we would redo, and what comes next

Three years of running inference at the edge, compressed into six regrets and four milestones.

  • Treat PoPs as cattle on day one. Hand-tuned sites cost us 14 incident-hours before provisioning was automated.
  • Measure queue delay, not utilisation. GPU utilisation sat at 90% while p99 was fine, and the reverse.
  • Ship the fallback first. Overflow to regional GPUs turned three Sev-1s into non-events.
  • Version models and runtimes together. Mismatched kernels caused 60% of our canary rollbacks.
  • Budget watts like milliseconds. Rack power, not rack space, is the scarce resource at a PoP.
  • Keep evals at the edge. Quality drift only showed up on real traffic, never on the offline suite.
